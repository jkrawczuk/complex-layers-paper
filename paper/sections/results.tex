\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Evaluation scope}
The final evaluation uses two datasets, three base learners, and two regularization regimes per learner.
For each dataset, \texttt{cpl}, \texttt{svm}, and \texttt{logreg} were calibrated separately to obtain
approximately sparse and dense first-neuron solutions. All results are based on repeated stratified
cross-validation with 10 folds and 10 repeats, with a maximum depth of 31 neurons.

\subsection*{Colon cancer benchmark}
On \texttt{colon\_cancer}, the sparse regime often benefited from the first few successive neurons.
For \texttt{cpl} with $C=0.03$, the mean vote accuracy increased from 0.8438 for the first neuron to
0.8498 at $n=4$, while the average number of features per neuron remained close to four
(4.28, 4.59, 4.05, and 3.51 for $n=1,\dots,4$).
For \texttt{svm} with $C=1.2$, accuracy increased from 0.8195 at $n=1$ to 0.8512 at $n=3$, with the
average neuron width growing from 4.83 to 8.01 features.
For \texttt{logreg} with $C=3.5$, the best result also appeared after several neurons rather than at the
first one, increasing from 0.8545 at $n=1$ to 0.8657 at $n=4$.

The denser regime produced a different pattern.
For \texttt{svm} with $C=6.0$ and \texttt{logreg} with $C=6.4$, the best mean accuracy was already achieved
by the first neuron (0.8769 and 0.8726, respectively), and additional neurons did not improve the model.
In these settings, the first neuron was substantially wider, selecting on average 24.72 features for
\texttt{svm} and 30.59 features for \texttt{logreg}; later neurons became even broader.
The main exception was \texttt{cpl} with $C=0.08$, where the mean vote accuracy improved from 0.8726 at
$n=1$ to 0.8921 at $n=3$, while the neuron width increased from 21.33 to 25.57 features.

The weighted vote based on training-fold balanced accuracy did not change the main ranking of the
\texttt{colon\_cancer} configurations. It matched the best unweighted result for dense \texttt{cpl}
(0.8921 at $n=3$), dense \texttt{svm} (0.8769 at $n=1$), and dense \texttt{logreg} (0.8726 at $n=1$).
The clearest gain was observed for sparse \texttt{svm}, where the weighted vote reached 0.8545 at $n=4$,
compared with the best unweighted value of 0.8512 at $n=3$. In contrast, sparse \texttt{logreg} decreased
from an unweighted best of 0.8657 at $n=4$ to a weighted-vote best of 0.8574 at $n=3$.

Taken together, the \texttt{colon\_cancer} results indicate that useful complementary signal often appears in
the first two to four neurons under stronger sparsity, whereas dense first-neuron solutions more often
concentrate the available predictive signal immediately.

\subsection*{Synthetic benchmark with known ground truth}
The synthetic benchmark makes it possible to distinguish predictive performance from true signal recovery.
Here, the most informative observation is that different learners distribute ground-truth signal across depth
in qualitatively different ways.

\toprule

\texttt{logreg} & 5.0 & 8.22 & 5 & 0.8500 & 0.3521 & 0.4306 \\
\texttt{logreg} & 9.2 & 28.09 & 1 & 0.8150 & 0.2033 & 0.7264 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

the table reports first-neuron feature precision and cumulative informative-feature recall after five neurons.}
\label{tab:synthetic_summary}

In the sparse regime, later neurons frequently improved predictive performance.
For \texttt{cpl} with $C=0.035$, accuracy increased from 0.7990 at $n=1$ to 0.8500 at $n=12$, while
the first neuron selected only 4.04 features on average.
Its first-neuron informative-feature precision was 0.3268, with feature recall 0.1042 and noise fraction
0.6732.
For \texttt{svm} with $C=2.0$, the best mean vote accuracy was 0.8417 at $n=11$, compared with 0.8200 at
$n=1$.
The first neuron selected 6.89 features on average, with precision 0.1881, recall 0.1058, and noise
fraction 0.8119.
For \texttt{logreg} with $C=5.0$, the best result occurred at $n=5$, improving from 0.7710 at $n=1$ to
0.8500 at $n=5$.

In the denser regime, the first neuron often captured a larger fraction of the true signal, but additional
neurons contributed less useful information.
This was clearest for \texttt{logreg} with $C=9.2$, where the first neuron already achieved the best mean
accuracy of 0.8150, selected 28.09 features on average, recovered 47.5\% of informative features, and
captured 71.1\% cumulative recall after only two neurons.
However, later neurons added almost no new informative signal, and performance gradually declined.
For \texttt{svm} with $C=3.5$, the first neuron was much less precise (precision 0.0680, noise fraction
0.9320), but cumulative informative-feature recall increased from 0.1525 at $n=1$ to 0.5608 at $n=3$ and
0.8367 by $n=5$.
For \texttt{cpl} with $C=0.05$, cumulative informative-feature recall increased from 0.1325 at $n=1$ to
0.4825 at $n=3$ and 0.6792 at $n=5$, but at the cost of broad and noisy neurons.

Weighted voting again gave a more nuanced picture than a uniform improvement. At full 100-split coverage, the
weighted vote matched the unweighted best for dense \texttt{svm} (0.8360 at $n=3$) and dense \texttt{logreg}
(0.8150 at $n=1$), but did not improve sparse \texttt{cpl}, sparse \texttt{svm}, or sparse \texttt{logreg}.
Some later synthetic depths produced higher weighted-vote values after many splits had already terminated
(for example sparse \texttt{logreg} at $n=5$), but these estimates were based on reduced coverage and are
therefore treated as diagnostic rather than primary performance estimates.

These synthetic results separate two modes of behavior.
Some models, especially denser \texttt{logreg}, concentrate a large fraction of the ground-truth signal in
the first neuron.
Other configurations, including sparse \texttt{cpl} and \texttt{svm}, recover informative structure more
gradually across successive neurons.

\subsection*{Built depth as a model property}
The nominal maximum depth was 31 neurons, but the realized depth depended strongly on the learner and
regularization.
On \texttt{colon\_cancer}, the realized depth ranged from 8 neurons for sparse \texttt{logreg} to 31 neurons
for dense \texttt{cpl} and dense \texttt{svm}.
On the synthetic benchmark, realized depth was markedly smaller, ranging from 5 neurons for sparse
\texttt{logreg} to 16 neurons for dense \texttt{svm}.
This supports the view that neuron depth is not only a reporting axis but also an empirical signature of how
aggressively each base learner exhausts the remaining feature space.
