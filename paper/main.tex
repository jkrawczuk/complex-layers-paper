\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.}
High-dimensional gene-expression datasets remain difficult classification benchmarks because they combine
very small sample sizes with thousands of correlated features. Sparse linear models can preserve
interpretability, but their predictive behavior depends on how sparsity is imposed and how successive feature
subsets are constructed. This study examines successive sparse formal neurons in a complex-layer construction,
focusing on how signal, sparsity, and noise evolve across neuron depth.

\textbf{Methods.}
We study two complementary benchmarks: a classical colon cancer microarray dataset with 62 samples, 2,000
features, and two diagnostic classes, and a synthetic binary dataset with known informative and noise
features. Three linear learners are evaluated within the same complex-layer protocol: \texttt{cpl}
(convex piecewise-linear; hinge loss with L1 regularization), \texttt{svm} (squared hinge loss with L1
regularization), and \texttt{logreg} (logistic loss with L1 regularization). In each layer, one sparse neuron is
trained and its selected features are removed before fitting the next neuron. Performance is assessed using
repeated stratified cross-validation. We summarize predictive accuracy, selected-feature counts, weighted voting
and, for the synthetic benchmark, feature recovery across neuron depth.

\textbf{Results.}
On colon cancer, low-feature first-neuron solutions often benefited from later neurons: \texttt{cpl} improved
from 0.7690 at $n=1$ to 0.7710 at $n=4$, and \texttt{svm} from 0.8538 to 0.8545 at $n=3$; low-feature
\texttt{logreg} reached its best value at the first neuron. In high-feature regimes, \texttt{cpl} and
\texttt{svm} improved over the first few neurons, whereas high-feature \texttt{logreg} reached 0.8667 at
$n=1$ and did not improve with depth.
A train-fold balanced-accuracy weighted vote gave similar conclusions and slightly improved low-feature
\texttt{logreg} on the synthetic benchmark (0.7678 at $n=2$). On the synthetic benchmark, low-feature
configurations of \texttt{cpl}, \texttt{svm}, and \texttt{logreg} reached their best unweighted accuracies at
$n=3$, $n=3$, and $n=2$, respectively. Ground-truth-aware metrics showed two modes: high-feature first
neurons recovered more informative signal at once, whereas lower-feature configurations distributed recovery
across successive neurons.

\textbf{Conclusions.}
The results indicate that useful signal is usually concentrated in the first few sparse neurons, but its
distribution across depth depends on the learner and feature-count regime. The complex-layer protocol therefore
provides a reproducible way to analyze layered sparse linear classifiers without assuming that deeper voting
improves monotonically.
\end{abstract}
